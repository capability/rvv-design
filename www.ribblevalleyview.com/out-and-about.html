The Ribble Valley

Witches, weavers and wide open views.

Walk in the footsteps of the Pendle witches, cycle a Tour of Britain stage and explore the famous villages, towns and historical features of the beautiful Ribble Valley — Clitheroe, Whalley, Ribchester, and the great hill of Pendle itself.

Forest of Bowland

312 square miles of quiet country.

Sitting on Crown land owned by the Duchy of Lancaster, the Forest of Bowland AONB stretches from Hurst Green in the south to Wennington in the north. Stone villages, some pre-historic in their bones, are scattered across the fells.

Nicknamed “the Switzerland of England”, it was designated an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty in 1964 — and still feels properly remote.

Lancashire, in full

From Morecambe Bay to Blackpool Tower.

From the unique and wildlife-rich Morecambe Bay in the north to the flat coastal plains around Ormskirk and the Ribble Estuary — and from world-famous Blackpool in the west to the undulating fells of Bowland and the Pennines in the east. Visitors are truly spoilt.

Eat & drink

The Ribble Valley eats well.

Old coaching inns, gastropubs, farm shops and bakeries — proper Lancashire food and a quiet rotation of Michelin guide entries.

  • ●Northcote — a long-standing Michelin-starred kitchen, 10 minutes away.
  • ●The Inn at Whitewell — fires, oak floors, fells out the window.
  • ●Bashall Barn — farm shop, café and ice cream, 7 minutes away.
  • ●Holmes Mill, Clitheroe — beer hall, deli, cinema and bowling.
